The storm glass

The glass had been clear all morning, which was how Mara knew her father was lying. He kept it on the sill of the lamp room where the light could get at it, a sealed bulb of water and crystals that was meant to cloud before weather, and it sat there as clear as a held breath while the sea went the colour of slate.

“It's a toy,” he said, when she asked. “Your grandmother's toy.” He did not look at it. He looked at the wick he was trimming, and the wick did not need trimming.

She had learned to read him the way she read the water: not by what moved, but by what refused to.

A place to start, a place to keep going, and a place to finish.

Most people are not missing talent. They are missing a system, a quiet room and permission. Parchemin supplies the first two and says the third out loud.

Start

  • Blank page first. Structure is there when you want it, and never required.
  • Story spark. A person, what they want, and the wall in the way.
  • Templates. Short story, three acts, the Hero's Journey, Save the Cat.
  • A nudge when you need one. Prompts by what kind of stuck you are. Suggestions only.

Keep going

  • A chapter plan. Goal, conflict, turn, point of view, setting, prop.
  • A cast with voices. Twenty-one voices in six families, cast against each other for friction.
  • The dialogue panel. Who wants what, a script draft, and the cover-the-names test.
  • Snapshots and a drawer. Save a draft before a rewrite. Nothing you cut is ever deleted.

Finish

  • An outline that reads itself back. Across to check a chapter, down to check the book.
  • A finish line. A novella in fifteen chapters, a novel in thirty. A date is a kindness, and you can pause it.
  • Today's words, no streaks. Days you wrote are shown. Days you didn't are just quiet.
  • Export. Markdown and EPUB today. A manuscript .docx is next.

The craft, built in.

One coherent method, assembled from the best writing teachers, that you learn by using it. The app gives you questions, patterns and checks. The answers stay yours.

  1. IdeaA story is a what-if: a person, a want, and a wall tied to the want. If nothing goes wrong, it is a situation.
  2. HeroWants one thing, needs another. A flaw that hurts them and someone else, which others see and they do not.
  3. PlotFifteen beats as a skeleton. Beats are not chapters; several can share one, and one can span many.
  4. VoiceA want, a fear and a way of seeing other people. Not an accent. Cast supporting characters against the hero.
  5. CastIncompatible wants, not opposite ones. Everyone needs a function nobody else in the book can serve.
  6. SettingDon't ask what a room looks like. Ask what makes it hard, and end with objects someone can pick up.
  7. ChaptersEach has a goal, a conflict and a turn. Know the last line before you write the first.
  8. DialogueNobody says what they mean. Every line is a tactic. Draft it as a script, cover the names, read it aloud.
  9. HabitA chapter a day. Start tomorrow with a sentence already waiting.

Credit goes to Jessica Brody (Save the Cat Writes a Novel), John Truby (The Anatomy of Story) and Robert McKee (Dialogue), to the screenplays of Aaron Sorkin and Quentin Tarantino, and to the teacher whose video series started it.

What Parchemin will not do.

The rules are short because they are meant to be kept.

  • Write the story for you.No generated chapters, no “fix my prose” button. There is no AI in the app. If an optional guide ever comes, it will run on a server you control, be off by default, and never put a word in your manuscript.
  • Harvest your work.No training on your text, no analytics on what you write, no selling of data. There is no server to send it to.
  • Gamify guilt.No streaks to lose, no shame notifications, no leaderboards. Encouragement has to be true.
  • Lock you in.Open export formats, a whole-library backup file, and a clean way out. The app keeps working with no account, no server and no internet.
  • Make structure mandatory.Every craft tool can be ignored. Blank page first, always.
  • Overclaim.We say what we believe, and we say it as a belief. No invented statistics, no inflated history.

Your words stay yours.

Everything lives in a folder on your computer. Each chapter is a plain Markdown file you can open with anything. A backup is one file you hold yourself, and Parchemin tells you plainly when it was last made and what has changed since.

Put the folder in your own Dropbox, iCloud or OneDrive and it follows you between machines. Parchemin itself never sends anything anywhere.

The code will be published as free software under the GNU Affero General Public License. Whoever hosts a changed copy will then have to offer you its source, so if this project ever stops, the app keeps working and the code stays yours to run.

How it is built

Parchemin is written and maintained with the help of AI coding tools, by one person, and we say so. The app you install contains no AI, and nothing you write ever reaches one. A founder's note on why will follow.
