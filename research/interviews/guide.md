# Writer interview guide

For 6 to 10 conversations of about 35 minutes. Purpose: learn what threads and papers cannot, namely **who Parchemin's users are and why they really stop, switch tools, or keep going**.

Parchemin is an early personal project. Say so. You are researching, not selling.

## 1. What this is for

Each part of the interview exists to answer one of the open questions in `research.md` section 9.3, so every minute has a decision attached.

| Open question | Interview section | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| Who are the real users? | 1, 8 (screener too) | Which forms and genres to design for first (novel, short story, poem, fan fiction, memoir) |
| Why do drafts actually stop? | 3 | Whether to build the end-of-session note, the Drawer, the middle check, and in what order |
| Do writers want trackers and reference cards inside the writing app? | 2, 4 | Reference cards, submission log, Sparks inbox |
| What do hobbyist, fan and disabled writers think of an opt-in questions-only assistant? | 7 | Whether to build one at all |
| Does a structured craft guide help people finish? | 3, 6 | The core product bet |
| How do they feel about streaks and numbers? | 2, 6 | Rhythm view versus the current "N days in a row" |
| What does a first-time, phone-only, or non-English writer need? | 4, 9 | Language, accessibility and mobile priorities |

## 2. Rules for the interviewer

These matter more than the questions.

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time you…" produces facts. "Would you use…?" produces polite guesses. Only the concept section (6) deals in hypotheticals, and it is read as a signal of reasons, never as demand.
2. **Never pitch.** Do not describe Parchemin's features before section 6. Do not defend an idea when someone dislikes it. A dislike is the finding.
3. **Do not lead.** Not "Don't you find streaks stressful?" but "How do you feel about tracking how much you write?"
4. **Silence is fine.** Wait five seconds after an answer. The second answer is usually the true one.
5. **Follow the story.** Useful probes: "Can you give me an example?", "What happened next?", "What did you do instead?", "What was that like?", "Why did that matter?"
6. **You are not their editor or therapist.** Do not give advice on their writing. Do not diagnose. Do not interpret feelings back at them.
7. **Do not promise features.** If asked: "I'm not sure yet. That's why I'm asking."
8. **Never ask to read their work.** If they offer text, decline it. You do not need it, and you should not keep it.
9. **Stop when someone is distressed.** Writing touches grief, memory, health and shame. Offer to skip, pause or end. Mean it, and do not probe harder.
10. **Say honestly what Parchemin is** if asked: a personal project, early, free so far, nothing is being sold or collected from them.

## 3. Who to talk to

Aim for variety, not representativeness. Eight conversations will not be a sample. They will show which problems are real and which were invented by the research.

Cover at least one of each, and let one person fill several rows:

- A writer who **stopped** a long project and hasn't returned
- A writer who **finished** something long, and how
- A **poet**
- A **fan fiction** writer
- A **short-story writer** who submits
- A **memoir or personal-essay** writer (handle section 3 gently)
- A **French-speaking** or otherwise multilingual writer
- A writer who mostly writes **on a phone** or in short bursts
- A writer who **uses ADHD, dyslexia or a chronic illness** as part of their writing life, if they offer it. Never ask anyone to disclose it
- A **hobbyist** and a **published** writer, so you don't hear only one kind of voice

**Where to find them:** people you know who write; friends of friends; a local library or community writing group (ask the organiser); online communities **with the moderator's permission**, never by cold-messaging individuals. Avoid close friends who will be kind to you. An introduction to a stranger is worth more.

**Screener** (ask by message before booking, five questions):
1. What do you write? (form and genre)
2. How long have you been writing?
3. Is there a piece you've stopped, finished, or are in the middle of?
4. What do you write on, mostly? (tool, device)
5. Would you be comfortable talking about that for 30 minutes?

## 4. Logistics, consent and privacy

- **Length:** 30 to 40 minutes. Video, phone or in person. Someone else's tool, someone else's time: be punctual.
- **Compensation:** optional. If you offer any (a coffee, a gift card), offer it regardless of what they say.
- **Recording:** only with explicit consent, and always offer "notes only". If you record, store it locally, write up within 24 hours, then delete it.
- **Anonymity:** label people W01, W02… in all notes. Keep the name-to-code list apart from the notes, or don't keep one.
- **Quotes:** record verbatim in notes. Do not put identifiable quotes in public or in the repo without their say-so.
- **Right to stop** at any time, and to have their notes deleted afterwards.

**Consent script (read aloud):**
> Thanks for doing this. I'm building a small writing app, still very early, and I'm trying to understand how people really write: what helps, and what gets in the way. I'm not selling anything and I won't ask to see your writing. You can skip any question or stop whenever you like. With your permission I'll take notes, and I'd like to record audio for my own use only, which I'd delete after writing up. Is that okay? And can I use short anonymous quotes in my notes?

## 5. The conversation

Timings are a guide. Skip freely. The order matters: concepts come late and AI comes after them so neither primes the stories.

### 1. Warm-up, about 4 minutes
- What do you write? What are you working on at the moment?
- How long have you been writing? How did you start?
- Tell me about the last thing you wrote that you were proud of. *(Let them choose "proud". Listen for why.)*

### 2. The last session, about 8 minutes
- Walk me through the last time you sat down to write. Where were you? What time? What did you open?
- What happened in the first ten minutes?
- When did it get hard, if it did? What did you do then?
- How did it end? How did you decide to stop?
- When you came back next, what was that like?
- *If tracking comes up:* tell me about how you keep track of how much you've written.

### 3. Stopping and finishing, about 8 minutes (the core of the interview)
- Tell me about a piece you stopped working on. What was it? When did you stop? What was happening in your life and in the piece?
- Looking back, what would have had to be different for you to keep going?
- Have you gone back to it? What happened?
- Now tell me about something you did finish. What was different?
- *Probe for specifics, not summaries:* "What was the day you stopped like?" is better than "Why do you think you stopped?"

### 4. Tools and keeping things, about 5 minutes
- What do you write in? Why that one?
- Have you ever switched? What made you switch, and what did you lose or gain?
- Have you ever lost writing, or been afraid of losing it? What happened? What do you do about backups now?
- Where do your ideas, notes and research live? How do you find them again?
- *Optional, with permission:* could you show me your setup? (Ask them to hide any text they don't want me to see.)

### 5. Other people, about 4 minutes
- Who sees your writing? Who doesn't?
- Tell me about the last time you shared something. How did it feel afterwards?
- Is there anything you wish you had in terms of readers or community?

### 6. Concept reactions, about 6 minutes
Show these as plain written cards, one at a time, not as a product demo. Ask about *their story from section 3*, not about the future.

| Card | Plain description to read out |
|---|---|
| A. End-of-session note | "When you stop, there's an optional spot to write what happens next. It shows up when you come back." |
| B. The Drawer | "Anything you cut goes to a drawer instead of being deleted. You can bring it back." |
| C. Rhythm instead of a streak | "It shows the days you've written, never a count that resets. Rest days are built in." |
| D. Read it back | "Your device reads your chapter aloud." |
| E. Reader packet | "You export a chapter with a few questions of your own to give a trusted reader." |
| F. Safekeeping | "It reminds you, quietly, when your work hasn't been backed up for a while." |
| G. Streak counter *(deliberate contrast)* | "It shows how many days in a row you've written." |

For each, ask: **"Thinking about the piece you stopped, would this have changed anything? Why or why not?"** Then: **"What do you do today instead?"** and **"What would worry you about it?"**

Finish with: **"If you could keep only two of these, which? Which would you be glad never to see?"**
*Card G is there to hear how people talk about streaks without leading them. Do not hint which card you hope they prefer.*

### 7. AI, about 3 minutes
Neutral, and asked after the concept cards.
- Has AI played any part in your writing, or in how you feel about writing? *(Let them define it. Do not prompt "ChatGPT".)*
- What would be fine and what wouldn't? What's the line for you?
- *Only if they mention it:* what if something only asked you questions about your writing and never wrote anything? What would you think of that? What would worry you?

### 8. Close, about 2 minutes
- Is there anything I haven't asked that I should have?
- Is there someone else you think I should talk to?
- Is it okay if I contact you with a follow-up question? Thank them.

### 9. Optional modules (swap in for 5 minutes)

- **Poets:** how do you decide where a line ends? How do you know a poem is finished? What happens to the shape of a poem when you move it between tools? How do you order a set of poems?
- **Fan writers:** how do you keep track of canon and where you diverge? What happens between drafting and posting? How do you feel about updates and comments?
- **Memoir writers (go gently):** how do you handle writing about real people? Is there material you keep separate or private? *Do not ask what the hard material is.*
- **Short-story submitters:** how do you keep track of where a story is? What's the most annoying part? Do you format it yourself?
- **Serial writers:** how do you decide when to post? What happens when you fall behind?
- **French or multilingual writers:** which language do you write in, and what happens when a tool assumes English? Does spacing before `: ; ! ?` matter to you? Quebec or France conventions?
- **Writers who live with ADHD, dyslexia, pain or fatigue** (only if they brought it up): what helps on a bad day? What do tools get wrong? *Never ask for a diagnosis.*

## 6. After each interview

1. **Within 24 hours**, fill in `notes-template.md` while memory is fresh.
2. **Separate observations from interpretations.** What they said and did is one column; what you think it means is another.
3. **Capture verbatim quotes** for the three or four moments that surprised you.
4. **Add a row to `tally.csv`** (segment, tools, which cards helped, reasons they gave).
5. **Delete the recording** once the notes are written.

## 7. After all of them

- **Look for patterns across at least three people** before calling anything a finding. One vivid story is a lead.
- **Saturation check:** after about the fifth interview, if the last two produced mostly stories you'd already heard, you have the main themes. If every conversation shows something new, keep going.
- **Write one page**: five or six findings, each with the quotes behind it and the decision it affects (use the table in section 1).
- **Update `research.md`:** section 9.3 questions, and the ranked opportunities in section 6.
- **Be honest in the write-up** about how few people you spoke to and how you found them.

## 8. Things to watch for in yourself

- **Hearing what you hoped to hear.** The easiest mistake. When someone says your idea is nice, ask what they do today instead. If the answer is "nothing", the problem may not exist.
- **Treating politeness as interest.** People rarely say "I would never use that". They say "Oh, that's interesting." Neither is a signal. Past behaviour is.
- **Filling silences with your own ideas.** Wait.
- **Talking about Parchemin.** If you are explaining the app, you have stopped researching.
